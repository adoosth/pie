# Review backlog

Words whose recorded ancestry has to be re-read before it may be counted.
Wiktionary records what an editor wrote; a word parked here is **not**
counted as explained, and none of its ancestry is used as evidence, until
someone opens the cited source and decides. The list is generated by
`python3 tools/backlog.py --write` from the corpus itself — no entry here is
typed by hand, and an entry that disagrees with its source leaves the file
only by a change in the data.

## Kinds

| kind | means |
|---|---|
| `etymon_missing` | the PIE (or oldest modelled) ancestor named in anc is not a headword in that stage's glossary |
| `cognate_conflict` | the entry's cognate set names a different PIE form from the PIE ancestor in anc |
| `hedged_ancestry` | ancestry is asserted although the source's own etymology hedges it (uncertain, unknown, possibly, perhaps, probably, disputed, doubtful) |
| `sense_mismatch` | the entry's gloss shares no content word with the claimed etymon's gloss or with its cognate set's gloss |

## What the kinds cannot say

`etymon_missing` applies when the named ancestor is not a headword in our
glossary, and that can be a gap in the glossary rather than a bad etymon:
the PIE lexicon holds a subset of the reconstructions the corpus cites, and
a proto-stage lexicon is smaller still. The reviewer's job is exactly that
distinction — open the source, and either the etymon is recorded (the
glossary needs the word) or it is not (the entry is wrong).

`sense_mismatch` compares glosses only where both sides have one, and
semantic development is broad, so it over-reports metaphor and metonymy.
It is a reading list, not a verdict, and no count of it belongs in a
published coverage number.
